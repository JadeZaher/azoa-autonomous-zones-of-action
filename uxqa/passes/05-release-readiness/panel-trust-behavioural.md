# Trust and behavioural panel

Pass05 demonstrates review, acknowledgment and import into an editable private canvas. It does not demonstrate a working economic process or transfer of the detailed brief to an implementer. The visible copy consistently describes planning notes and external/manual work. The main trust question has therefore moved from “why is no draft available?” to “what does the author believe this imported artifact preserves and accomplishes?” Accessibility defects also limit who can independently inspect the result before trusting it.

This panel read only the UX study, pass05 scenario/personas, aggregate report, AI lane report, selected `ux.json` summaries and three screenshots: `planning-draft-review.png`, `review-acknowledged-before-import.png` and `imported-canvas-settled.png`. It inspected no application source, trace, credential/session files, private raw intake/transcript, raw provider/network artifacts or executable flow files, and made no browser/provider calls. Evidence paths below are relative to `uxqa/passes/05-release-readiness/`; no raw brief, transcript, screenshot or tenant identifier is republished. Proposed human sessions have not occurred.

## Observations and boundaries

`ux-report.json` records nine raw runs: eight completed UI journeys and one separate probe. All three navigation gold tasks passed, and coverage remains 12/57 pages (21.1%). Friction is 1.875 per UI journey versus 1.5 in pass04: a 25% increase, failing the configured 10% tolerance. The `newHigh` check remains unassessed. Completion of the limited tasks cannot override those open gates or establish human comprehension.

At `/actions`, `flows/tenant-workflow-ai/lane-report.md` reports one consented submission and exactly one observed interview request, with no retry. The generic harness count of zero API calls is a coverage limitation; it is not zero provider use. The refined fictional brief supplied the finer operational evidence and settlement actors requested previously. The response offered a planning draft without repeated operational blockers. This is evidence that one refined input produced a draft, not a controlled demonstration of the cause of pass04's refusal or a completed second turn of that earlier session.

The draft review screenshot explicitly distinguishes planning handoff data from proof of review, approval, compliance or settlement. It discloses nine Emit nodes and the configurations for review. The lane report records eight sequential Control edges, no embedded detailed amounts/allocations/timing/approval rules, and a warning to preserve those decisions separately for implementation/review. Import was disabled before acknowledgment. The checked acknowledgment screenshot and run steps show review followed by acknowledgment and import. The imported screenshot shows 9 nodes and 8 edges, a selected settlement-note node, editable fields and an untouched Create Action control. These observations support a limited review/import outcome. They do not establish saving, persistence, export, notification delivery, external review or payment success.

The acknowledgment and status copy are useful safeguards: import replaces the canvas without execution; a later explicit run may enqueue configured tenant webhook notifications; checkpoints neither wait for nor prove review/approval. The imported status says nothing has moved funds, settled, been published or run. The lane reports named-provider consent before submission and no generated statutory rates, thresholds or legal/tax determinations. None of those observations certifies substantive advice accuracy, privacy implementation or informed human consent.

The AI run's first axe scan was clear. Its selected-node scan reports two unnamed editable inspector controls (`label`, critical) and Delete Node contrast (`color-contrast`, serious, 3.34:1 versus 4.5:1 according to the lane). The screenshot visibly places the first node underneath the floating toolbar. The lane reports a normal first-node click timing out and successful selection of a middle node. This detour occurred outside the attempt wrapper, so zero recorded failed attempts understates the actual interaction difficulty. No delete or field edit occurred.

Across the UI runs, three axe rule instances represent two unique rule IDs and four affected nodes: two inspector controls, Delete Node and one contrast node in the audit gold run. The audit node is not identified by that run's `ux.json`; it must not be assumed to be Delete Node or a particular audit text element. The separate operator-readiness audit scan was clear. These are differing observations, not evidence that the audit finding was disproved.

## Five-session-per-segment validation plan

The following sessions are proposed, not completed. Use synthetic recorded screens and briefs first. Do not consume additional provider reservations; any future live authoring requires separate authorization. Do not save, run, notify, submit identity information, change authority or move value. Collect unaided choices before neutral retrospective questions such as “What on the screen led you to that?” Record confidence separately from correctness.

| Segment: five sessions each | Task and measures | Confirmation | Refutation or material weakening |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepared tenant authors; TB05-1 and TB05-5 | Review a fictional detailed brief, offered generic graph and imported status. Ask what information is retained, what must be preserved separately and how they would pause or hand work over. Measure expected artifact contents, next action, missing information and confidence. | Authors assume amounts/rules/actors are retained or confuse imported with saved; their proposed handoff omits essential decisions. | All five accurately locate the limitation, preserve the separate brief and distinguish imported from durable saved state without assistance. This does not validate persistence behavior. |
| Receiving workflow implementers/reviewers; TB05-1 and TB05-2 | Give the synthetic scaffold and the separately preserved brief, then ask recipients to identify implementation duties, evidence, approvals and settlement work. Measure whether they can separate notes from executable rules and detect absent detail. | Recipients treat sequential notes as implemented approvals/payment or cannot reconstruct responsibilities from the available handoff. | All five correctly identify external controls, evidence and owners and use the separate brief without re-inventing decisions. Actual live handoff effectiveness remains untested. |
| Novice or plain-language workflow designers; TB05-2 and TB05-3 | Explain checkpoint completion, import, later run and consent stages in their own words. Ask when data may leave for a provider or a configured tenant webhook and whether approvals become proven. Measure teach-back, lookup and false certainty. | Participants predict payment/control enforcement or assume one consent covers every later action without understanding the stated boundaries. | All five distinguish disclosed provider submission, non-executing canvas replacement and possible future notifications, and deny unsupported enforcement/proof. Copy comprehension does not prove data handling. |
| Keyboard/screen-reader workflow authors; TB05-4 | In a separately authorized safe replica of the recorded state, inspect a node and distinguish name/configuration and destructive controls before acknowledging review. Measure field identification, focus, assistance and whether the acknowledgment reflects real inspection. | Missing names prevent independent distinction or push participants to acknowledge without meaningful inspection. | All five reliably identify and inspect the intended fields without help using available cues. Recorded missing-label defects still require technical accessibility review even if a workaround succeeds. |
| Low-vision/zoom workflow authors; TB05-4 and TB05-5 | Inspect the first and middle node at usual zoom, locate deletion/undo and explain unsaved versus retained state without activating destructive or save actions. Measure visibility, mistaken selection, detours and confidence. | Toolbar overlap or low-contrast controls obstruct intended inspection/control, or a populated canvas leads to unsupported persistence assumptions. | All five can inspect the desired nodes, identify control consequences and state what still needs saving unaided. A successful simulation does not establish actual save/recovery correctness. |

Five-person qualitative groups can expose mechanisms; they do not estimate population failure rates. Support each confirmed mechanism with an actual observed decision and its screen cue. Reject or narrow hypotheses when participants consistently understand the existing boundaries, and report accessibility barriers separately even if users compensate successfully.

## Implication for synthesis

Preserve pass03/pass04 partial outcomes and count pass05 only as a completed limited planning review/import. The new artifact makes it possible to research whether honest disclaimers, acknowledgment and generic planning notes support the intended handoff; it does not answer that question. Complete task predicates, zero dead clicks and one successful pointer path cannot establish informed consent, accessible authoring, durable storage, external approval, legal/tax correctness or payment success. The failed friction trend, unassessed new-high check and recorded accessibility defects preclude treating this panel as clean release or two-pass approval.
