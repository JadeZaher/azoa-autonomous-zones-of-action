# Evidence boundary

This lens read only this pass's scenario, personas, four lane reports, `ux-report.json`, and the three gold-run `ux.json` files. It did not inspect application source, other panel reports, browser state, credentials, session files, flows or traces. Paths below are relative to `uxqa/passes/01-release-readiness/`. Screenshots named in lane reports are evidence references rather than independently inspected visual evidence.

These are hypotheses about human trust and decisions. Successful agent navigation does not establish human comprehension, informed consent, safe authority selection or acceptable legal/tax advice. This pass never exercised an AI workflow designer, value movement, identity-document submission, administrative writes, recovery credentials, real expired-session recovery or the owner's root-key drill. No fixes are verified here.

# What the observations support

The corrected mobile journey reached ordinary sign-in, used its visible operator-sign-in link, and reached the operator recovery explanation. That is useful evidence of a visible handoff. The anonymous gold task redirected from `/operator/audit` to `/operator/login` in three seconds without an input or click. It proves a route-level sign-in destination, not that a person understood who owns the credentials or could recover access. The 401 on the anonymous session request is consistent with the observed signed-out state and is not evidence that recovery failed.

The authenticated operator found readiness, audit, tenants and providers with three clicks and no failed attempt. The screen distinguished this-browser logout from global revocation. Those are promising privacy and control cues. Neither revocation action was activated, permission denial was not induced, and the audit list was empty. Consequently this lane cannot establish that people understand revocation scope, that authorization is correct, or that an empty audit proves an absence of all activity.

Both account lanes reached their destinations, but both described the same duplicate raw chain-fetch errors. The zoom lane also encountered a nameless menu and dense settings content. Successful navigation alongside these signals can conceal uncertainty about whether it is safe to continue. No participant was asked to make that judgment.

# Measurement cautions affecting trust conclusions

- The initial mobile `ABANDONED` marker arose from a strict locator matching two LOGIN elements before any click. Its corrected journey completed with two clicks, no failed attempt and no workaround. Preserve both runs, but do not attribute that abandonment or the home-route friction peak to human mistrust or inability to find login.
- `gold-member-settings` is recorded PARTIAL after one successful attempt. Its `ux.json` contains a Settings click followed by navigation to `/settings`, zero errors, no abandonment and no workaround. This is compatible with the coordinator's reported timing/predicate issue. It is not evidence that a person failed to find settings. Preserve the failed gold outcome until a corrected, separately identified run satisfies its completion condition.
- Two completed gold tasks are small route-level checks: operator recovery did not exercise authentication or actual credential recovery; audit did not inspect a populated history. Their success rates do not establish the corresponding trust outcomes.
- Coverage is 11/57 pages (19.3%), below the configured 20% target. Zero unmatched paths supports the inventory mapping, not feature completeness. The account API observer does not cover every backend request, so zero matched calls is not proof that personal information was never transferred.
- Reported contrast, unnamed-menu and focusability issues may impair access to reassurance or recovery cues. This lens does not duplicate a full accessibility assessment or infer a privacy breach from axe findings. The pointer zoom persona opened the nameless menu; a screen-reader user's experience remains untested.
- The report's friction comparison uses the setup smoke as its previous pass. Different tasks and route coverage make that unsuitable as evidence of a worsening human experience. There is no matched prior research pass or two-pass confirmation.
- No observed dark pattern, unauthorized disclosure or unexpected write is established by these read-only journeys. Their absence in a small agent sample is not a clearance of the untested consent and authority boundaries.

# Human validation plan

Recruit five people for each affected segment below. These are proposed future sessions, not completed research. Use a marked disposable environment, synthetic account details and no real identity documents, money, owner root keys or production credentials. Observe unaided first; ask neutral retrospective questions after the task. Stop before submitting a document, changing permissions, saving provider configuration or revoking real sessions.

Any failed comprehension question should be followed by “What on the screen led you to that?” rather than teaching the desired answer. Report individual paths and quotes with consent; do not convert a five-person qualitative sample into a population failure rate. Consider severity escalation only after observing concrete goal failure or consequential decisions. Keep role, disability and connectivity effects separate where the evidence supports them.

# Implication for synthesis

Prioritize a clearer explanation of unavailable chain information and validation of account-control scope (TB-03 and TB-05). Preserve the useful existing role handoff, provider/recipient wording and session-scope cues while testing whether people understand them. Provider freshness and owner-mediated recovery are specific open questions rather than demonstrated blockers. This pass supplies no evidence that the proposed tenant AI interview, legal/tax warnings, model consent or draft-only behavior works; add those surfaces to a separately guarded future scenario before making a trust claim about them.
